# Flux 1.7 — 2026-06-15

Two additions, both about looking further — forward in time on the Dashboard, and backward in time on History. The Dashboard now projects where the battery's charge will land by the end of the off-peak window, and History gains previous/next navigation so you can step back through past weeks and months instead of only the windows ending today.

## What's New

### Off-peak charge projection (T-1533)

While the battery is charging during the off-peak window, the Dashboard hero's charging line now shows the state-of-charge the battery is expected to reach by the time the window ends — for example `Charging · 4.50 kW · ~99% by 14:00`. It answers the question you actually have during off-peak: "will this top up to where I want it before the cheap rate ends?"

The projection is computed on the backend from an idealised two-rate charge curve — 4.5 kW up to 95%, then a 500 W taper to 100% — and clamped between the current state-of-charge and 100%. It reuses the same battery capacity the existing cutoff estimate uses, so the two figures never disagree. It is a best-case estimate: it assumes the battery keeps charging at the modelled rate and is deliberately independent of the live battery power reading and of any active Dashboard simulation.

The figure appears only inside the off-peak window, only when there is fresh live data, and only while the battery is actually charging. Outside those conditions nothing is shown, so the charging line stays clean. iOS and macOS.

### History period navigation (T-1497)

The History screen's **Wk** (week) and **Mo** (month) ranges gain a period header so you can move through time. Previous/next chevrons step one week or month at a time, a tappable label opens a date picker to jump straight to a given week or month, and a **Current** button between the chevrons returns you to the period ending today. The date picker follows your system's first-day-of-week setting, and on macOS the ← and → keys move between periods.

Past periods are served from stored data through a new date-range request to the backend, so browsing history is fast and doesn't recompute live figures. When a past period is only partly covered by stored data, the header shows an "N of M days" subtitle so you know the totals are based on a partial period. A past period with no data at all shows a distinct no-data notice with the cards still in place, rather than disappearing. iOS and macOS.

## Upgrade Notes

No breaking changes. This release pairs the iOS/macOS app with a backend update: the off-peak charge projection is computed on `GET /status`, and past-period History browsing uses a new date-range form on `GET /history`. The existing `days=N` history requests are unchanged and behave exactly as before. Both additions are read-only — they only ever display a projection or stored history and never write to your battery, inverter, or the backend's data.
